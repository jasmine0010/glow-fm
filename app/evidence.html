Results

Floods under forest, seen by NISAR: every test, including the failures

Abstract

Four things to know first. Scores are CSI, the flooded pixels correctly mapped, divided by those plus misses and false alarms, where 1 is perfect.

Figures

Methods

Data

NISAR Level-2 GCOV backscatter (HH and HV, provisional release CRID P05023, 40 MHz scenes), resampled to a 50 m grid. Each flood pass is compared with earlier passes on the same track. Land cover is ESA WorldCover 2021, ground height is USGS 3DEP lidar (Copernicus GLO-30 and FABDEM V1-2 in the tests without lidar), river levels are USGS gauges, rainfall is IMERG V07 Late Run, and the official flood map is Copernicus Global Flood Monitoring (GFM).

GLOWfm's method (version 4, frozen)

  • Baseline. The per-pixel median of every same-track scene within 60 days of the flood pass, excluding the flood date, where at least three values exist.
  • Signal. The change in HH backscatter (terrain-flattened γ⁰, as NISAR GCOV delivers it), smoothed over 3×3 pixels. If higher valley forest (4–8 m above the river) brightened by more than 0.5 dB, as rain on the canopy does, the signal becomes the HH change minus the HV change.
  • Where it looks. Tree cover, herbaceous wetland and other land cover under 6 m above the river profile and within 4 km of the valley line.
  • Cut-off. Otsu's split of the signal over that valley floor, at least 0.5 dB. It is set by each scene and uses no reference data.
  • Abstains where the nearest gauge is more than six days past its crest.

Trust light

Green when low valley forest (under 2 m above the river) brightened more than higher valley forest by more than 0.5 dB, and the pass wasn't rainy (at least 5 mm in the prior 24 hours or 25 mm in 72 hours). Red otherwise. The rule was fixed before its first fair test, where it was right on 3 of 4 judgeable passes (Fig. 6d).

The reference ("the river's answer")

Water levels from USGS gauges at the time of the pass are interpolated along the valley and compared with lidar ground height. A pixel is flooded where the water stands more than 0.5 m above the ground, and dry where the ground is 0.5–5 m above the water. Pixels in between are left unlabelled. Labels are kept only within a 4 km valley corridor, where flooding connects to the river's reference water, within 10 km of a gauge along the valley, and outside permanent water. It is an estimate, not an observation of water. Fig. 10 tests it by hiding gauges and, post hoc, by moving its water surface; Table 6 checks it against photos.

Scoring

Critical success index, CSI = hits / (hits + misses + false alarms), on mapped, gauge-supported pixels outside permanent water. 95% intervals come from a block bootstrap with 2 km blocks along the valley (2,000 resamples, seed 0); differences between maps use paired resamples. Post hoc, the same intervals were recomputed with 5 km and 10 km blocks, since flood extent is correlated over longer distances; most widen (Fig. 5 caption, scripts/posthoc_reference_checks.py). A pass is judged when the reference holds at least 500 ha of flooding.

Frozen one-time tests

Before each test, its protocol, scene list, site files, run code and scientific modules were hashed (SHA-256) into a receipt. The run refuses to start if any of them has changed, and refuses a second attempt. Results are written once; a fix after scoring is labelled post hoc and doesn't replace the first number. Readings were fixed in each protocol before the data was opened, including what would count as a failure.

Without lidar

Two frozen tests replaced lidar ground height in GLOWfm with global elevation data, first Copernicus GLO-30, a surface model that includes treetops, then FABDEM V1-2, the same model with forests and buildings removed by a trained correction. Everything else in version 4 stayed fixed, including its 6 m limit, and each was scored on the same pixels against the same reference. A height map with no radar (land under 2 m above the river, by the same elevation data) was scored alongside. The bare-earth test fixed in advance a check that any gain didn't come from mapping more ground the reference can't label, and predicted its cause, that flooded forest should sit near the river by FABDEM. On the Satilla it sat a median 0.39 m above the river, against 4.92 m by Copernicus and −0.39 m by lidar.

Coverage and delay (Fig. 11)

For every flood GDACS reported in a 60-day window, NASA's CMR catalog was asked for each NISAR GCOV scene covering the reported point while the flood was reported, from both the provisional and the final collection. Each scene keeps its recording time and its first "Insert" date in the catalog; the gap between them is the wait before anyone can use it. Built by scripts/build_coverage.py; it describes NISAR's first season as the catalog showed it on the day it ran.

Water level under the canopy (Fig. 12)

An exploratory check, made after the frozen tests and not scored. NISAR's GUNW product compares the radar phase of two passes 12 days apart. Where flooded swamp stays coherent, a change in phase along the swamp is a change in water level, about 16 cm per cycle at this incidence. Three pairs over the Choctawhatchee River delta were read, a dry-season control and two pairs spanning Hurricane Isaias, seen from opposite directions. Phase steps were added up from west to east over coherent pixels and set to zero at the FDEP gauge at Bunker (CTBF1). Built by scripts/posthoc_swamp_fringes.py; the feasibility notes, with the gauge readings, are in research/FEASIBILITY.md.

Descriptive figures

Figs. 2, 3, 4 and 6 were computed after the tests from the saved pixel layers of the twelve frozen version-4 runs. The build checks them against the frozen records (pooled counts equal each run's scores, medians and ROC areas match the stored values) and nothing was re-scored or re-tuned.

The figure data is built by scripts/build_figures.py, which checks it against the frozen records before writing it, and every figure above downloads with the data it plots.
