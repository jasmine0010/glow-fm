How to read this dashboard

  • NISAR's map is the newest available pass, and river levels are live. Levels come straight from USGS. NISAR passes every few days, its scenes reach NASA's catalog days later, and this site's radar maps aren't updated automatically.
  • The flooding shown is an estimate. It compares river level with lidar ground height, within 10 km of a gauge. It isn't radar, and it isn't a sighting of water.
  • "What if" isn't a forecast. It adds the same rise to every gauge on every river. Real floods don't rise evenly.
  • How scores work. The match score runs from 0 to 1 and compares NISAR's map with the estimate at the same moment. It only counts ground the estimate can clearly call flooded or dry, so the two areas shown can differ even when the score is high. In a fair test, GLOWfm was fixed before the flood's data was opened, and the results page calls these frozen tests. Development scores come from rivers that helped build GLOWfm, so they flatter it. A river's fair test may have used an earlier method, so it can differ from the current method's score.
  • The switch on the map picks what it shows. NISAR shows NISAR's flood map; Estimate shows the gauge-and-lidar estimate; Both lays the estimate in see-through cyan over NISAR's magenta, so blue is where they agree, magenta where only NISAR mapped flooding and cyan where only the estimate did.
  • The blind-spot map is a layer. It's strongest zoomed out and fades to a faint trace over a tested flood; the switch in Map layers turns it off. The Blind spots tab checks any town, river or country. It shows where river floodplains sit under trees, using a modeled 100-year flood map and land cover. It marks where a flood could be hidden, not where one is. A place reads as high risk when at least half of its mapped floodplain within about 25 km is under trees and that covers at least 100 km², and moderate from a fifth and 25 km². Those cutoffs are our own.
  • Roads under water. The card counts drivable roads from OpenStreetMap on the ground the estimate floods, leaving out bridges and footpaths, and how much of it lies where Copernicus GFM's mask says today's flood map doesn't map. Mapped roads can be incomplete, and a raised road can stay dry where the ground beside it floods.
  • Don't use this for emergency decisions. Follow official warnings. See the results page for how well each part has held up.
