        How it works, and the data

        The area excludes permanent lakes and rivers. US floods are graded against the river's gauges and lidar; elsewhere there's no ground truth, and the trust light decides.

        The trust light is also on trial. Before NISAR acquired them, we sealed which of its next passes over US floods will be scored, and what the light predicts for each (Table 5 in the results).

        Floods the job looked at but couldn't prepare (0), and why:

          GLOWfm compares NISAR's pass during the flood with earlier passes on the same track, keeps forest and wetland close to the river, and lets the scene set its own cut-off. Its rules are in the results and in research/LIVE_PROTOCOL.md; since protocol v2 (research/LIVE_PROTOCOL_V2.md), floods outside the US use bare-earth heights from FABDEM, which tested better than treetop heights without lidar. Data: NISAR GCOV (NASA/ISRO, via ASF), NOAA river gauges, USGS river lines, gauges and 3DEP lidar, ESA WorldCover 2021, Copernicus GLO-30, FABDEM V1-2 (University of Bristol; Hawker et al. 2022; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, height layers made from it are shared under the same licence for non-commercial use; produced using Copernicus WorldDEM-30 © DLR e.V. 2010-2014 and © Airbus Defence and Space GmbH 2014-2018 provided under COPERNICUS by the European Union and ESA; all rights reserved), GDACS. Imagery © Esri.

          Roads under water are drivable roads from OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL), with footpaths and bridges left out, counted on the ground GLOWfm flags. They're saved with each prepared flood (scripts/build_live_roads.py); a run of your own asks OpenStreetMap for its box. Mapped roads can be incomplete, and a raised road can stay dry where the ground beside it floods.
